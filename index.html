Data Sources

  • Mortality: CDC NCHS VSRR provisional age-adjusted death rates, 12 months ending 2023 Q1 (firearm, homicide, drug overdose).
  • Demographics & socioeconomics: U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2022 1-year (income, race, poverty, education, unemployment, single-parent households).
  • Health: CDC BRFSS 2022 (heavy drinking and depression prevalence).
  • Gun policy: GIFFORDS Law Center Annual Gun Law Scorecard 2022 (grades and ranks).
  • Gun ownership: RAND state-level household firearm ownership estimates.
  • Inequality & demographics: Census ACS Gini coefficient (2019), veteran population share (2021).
  • Religion: Pew Research Center Religious Landscape Study 2023-24 (high religiosity score).

What is this?

This interactive map shows gun violence rates by state. You can adjust for confounding factors to see which states have higher or lower rates than expected.

What are confounders?

Confounders are factors that correlate with both the outcome (gun violence) and might explain some of the variation. For example, poverty rates differ by state and correlate with violence.

What is R² (variance explained)?

R² tells you how much of the state-to-state variation in gun violence is "explained" by the selected factors. An R² of 60% means these factors account for 60% of why some states have higher rates than others.

What are residuals?

When confounders are selected, the map shows residuals: positive (red) means more violence than expected, negative (green) means less. A state with +5.0 has 5 more deaths per 100k than predicted by the model.

Interpreting results

If a state ranks poorly in raw rates but improves after adjustment, its violence may be largely explained by demographics. If it remains an outlier, other factors (policy, culture, access) may be at play.
